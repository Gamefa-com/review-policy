“Simplicity is the ultimate sophistication.” With this brief phrase, da Vinci, over the course of an immortal endeavor, grasped that delivering the simplest explanations requires entangling yourself in invisible labyrinths.

Reviewing a video game, in the same way, presents itself at every turn as though there’s nothing particularly complicated about it, and everything happens in the simplest form possible. But let’s add an error of understanding to our error of sight and agree that reviewing a game is incredibly simple! Viewed head-on, a video game review is a straight line. Shift the camera angle slightly, and the monster behind it comes into view.

So if someone claims a task is infinitely simple and you happen to be a lazy person, run for the hills! Of course, these claimants aren’t entirely wrong. The simplicity usually plays out on the user’s or player’s side of things. But on the other side, where you stand, you must sprint past visible and invisible walls, and past the people who insist there’s nothing complicated about it, before you arrive at a high-quality, “clean” review.

Gamefa critics, regardless of background or circumstance, receive the necessary guidance for reviewing a video game before starting their work, so they don’t turn this simple process into something more complicated than it is!

This guidance is always ongoing, but most Gamefa users sit on the other side of the curtain, occasionally turning over in their minds the question: what gives critics the right to press the sacred keys of the keyboard and pass judgment on a video game in a historic court of law? Nothing! They’re simply users who call themselves critics and believe they know how to review a video game well!

Do you see this same belief within yourself? Set the misunderstanding aside, and this time get past the optical illusion too. You’re thinking correctly! All it takes is the will for the sacred keys to be granted to you. So, to perform this simple task, what complexities stand in your way as you seek to join the ranks of the experts?

The article ahead is a complete guide to conducting a standard video game review, at the very least the one Gamefa follows. Although some of these topics aren’t accepted by every critic active in the field, this article has tried to address the fundamentals of the craft from a global perspective.

## Playing the Game, Knowledge, and Growth

Experiencing the work must, without question, be the first and central component of reviewing a video game. A critic must manage every future branch of writing a review based on the experience they’ve had actually playing the game. You can’t expect someone to review a game they haven’t played and still consider themselves a critic.

Reviewing a video game through other people’s texts and opinions, videos, and so on isn’t really a review at all. The writer has merely offered an opinion about the game, an average of the sources they used. So right here, we need to understand what a review actually is.

As the name suggests, a review is an evaluation of the quality of a product, event, or similar, in which a skilled person describes its strengths and weaknesses to an audience in various ways. It’s worth noting that this definition doesn’t strictly hold for both words “critique” and “review,” but what we mean in this article is the common form used in the video game industry.

While derivative texts can also accomplish this task, republishing other people’s opinions cannot deliver the variety and precision that reviews require.

When you set out to experience a game, an opportunity arises: to convey a standard experience to others, calibrated by your own skill. Along the way, you mustn’t forget the role of skill. Unconditionally, you must possess greater skill, experience, and understanding of a video game than ordinary audiences do.

That doesn’t mean you must have worked in this field for decades, but the elements required to start are essential. If you can’t grasp the mechanics and fundamentals of a review, which we’ll get to shortly, not even dozens of hours and dozens of playthroughs will make your fingers flow.

On the other hand, even if you have solid experience, skill, and understanding of the video game industry, its genres, and related matters, you must continuously update your knowledge. Many of today’s trends weren’t broadly popular or widespread three years ago.

As a result, you must know every part of this field far better than your own audience, and through constant progress, maintain that gap. Your knowledge, skill, and passion must, at any given moment, exceed those of most amateur gamers, in relation to both the entire history of the video game industry and its contemporary history.

Now, to clear up any ambiguity: you don’t need to know everything, to be constantly playing games across every genre, or even to have a hand in making games! The real question is whether you genuinely care about, and believe in, improving. If the answer is yes, and you dedicate time to improvement at necessary and regular intervals, you can grow and evolve alongside this field’s breathtaking day-to-day progress.

Armed with this motivation and the necessary knowledge of the world of video games, your experience of a game transforms, simultaneously, from an ordinary daily pastime into a moment-by-moment examination. To examine each part of a game, you must be familiar with its genre. The following are the main video game genres:

- Action
- Adventure
- Shooter
- Role-playing
- Racing
- Sports
- Platforming
- Strategy
- And more…

Reading the [**All Video Game Genres**](https://diginoy.com/161985/game-develompent-3-all-genres/) article will help you get better acquainted with this classification and its subcategories. Those subcategories are always expanding, which is yet another solid reason for you to keep upgrading your knowledge.

Reading the [**Game Development Tutorial**](https://diginoy.com/tag/game-development/) series is another way to grow your knowledge of the field, but don’t settle for these suggestions. If you’re a regular follower of this space, you know that the ways of expanding your knowledge of video games go far beyond what we’ve proposed.

Even with respectable knowledge, you must spend at least a considerable amount of time with a title in order to grasp its dimensions, the development team’s goals, its quality, its strengths and weaknesses, and the logical reasons behind all of them across the story, gameplay, visual and audio presentation, replay value, and so on.

A quick glance at a game is just as harmful as not playing it at all. Many games reveal their core features, and more, only after the first third of their runtime. So we recommend that, under all circumstances, you play at least one-third to one-half of a game before reviewing it. Under more optimal conditions, when the critic has more time, that amount should extend to the end of the game.

That said, exceptions exist; for example, a full playthrough to reach the main ending screen of certain RPG titles takes dozens of hours.

In such scenarios, you shouldn’t cling to this article’s defaults. You must have the power to decide and to read the situation. If you feel that playing a certain amount of the game will reveal nearly all of its facets to you, there’s no need to play day and night to finish it so you can complete the writing stage on schedule.

While playing, many of the points mentioned above, and many more we’ll cover below, will dance before your eyes, slip through your fingers, and whisper in your ear. But there’s no guarantee you’ll remember them.

Note-taking is a miracle. You’ll believe in it once you try it. Believe it or not, even if you’re a one-of-a-kind genius, you’ll still forget some points by the time you sit down to write. Use pen and paper, your phone, your computer, a voice recorder, or even footage captured on your platform to record the positive and negative points of your experience with a game.

## The Review Framework

After spending a reasonable amount of time with a game’s gameplay, it’s time to write the review. Under certain conditions, some critics begin writing as they approach the end of their time with the game.

If the critic’s opinion shifts by the end of the playthrough, they can revise the text, but the optimal approach is to finish the necessary playthrough, or at least a reasonable portion of it, for reasons explained in the previous section.

The main sections of a video game are listed below. For at least the past two decades, this classification has remained largely valid and reliable.

- Story, plot, and characterization
- Gameplay
- Visuals
- Audio
- Fun factor, replay value, and innovation

While playing, you must recognize each of these categories explicitly and without ambiguity, and be able to tell them apart. Video games, and certain titles in particular, are highly cohesive experiences whose threads aren’t easily separated, but you must accept that risk and use your creativity to critique them.

Of course, not every game boils down to just these sections. Reviews of technical aspects and other parts appear in plenty of critiques too. Some critics believe performance, technical points, and certain other review components shouldn’t be treated separately from the fundamental framework of a game outlined above.

Most of the time, in story-driven games, a standard review begins with the “Story, Plot, and Characterization” section. It’s expected that immediately after addressing the story, the “Gameplay” section is taken up, and the article then follows the hierarchy described above.

Even so, the order of a review is debatable and can change depending on the content and the game itself. We recommend the standard, accepted order above, but given that some parts (like story) are absent from certain games, or differ in importance, no truly fixed order or framework can be prescribed. Don’t forget: attracting readers and earning their trust in your text and review has definite principles. Honor them, and creativity makes everything possible.

So treat this framework as the core of your review. The sections listed are exactly what you should be taking notes on. While note-taking, you don’t need to fully separate them. You simply need to record your points precisely and professionally so that later you can fit them into the main framework of a video game and carry your experience from your mind and your paper into the digital world, or another sheet of paper.

## The Skills of Criticism

But how is that possible? What should you take notes on, and how should you present them? Before diving into the framework itself, we need to look at a critic’s skill in wielding it.

Description, analysis, judgment, and recommendation are the four core skills of a critic, a point on which virtually all critics agree. Reviewing a topic, a section, a seemingly worthless fragment, or an unforgettable piece of a work must never be taken lightly.

If a part of a game looks worthless on the surface, you must provide a solid reason for your view. You think another part is revolutionary and impactful? Without a reason, your words are just everyday praise that any fan or zealot reaches for daily. But when you explain the cause, you’ve presented evidence that validates your assessment.

In many cases, the critic actually does have a convincing reason for a game’s strengths and weaknesses but simply can’t deliver it properly to the reader. Leaning on adjectives like masterpiece, disaster, beautiful, ugly, good, bad, and the like usually signals a critic’s weakness in analysis and in presenting causes. Of course, using such adjectives sparingly and sensibly, alongside a proper cause-and-effect relationship, isn’t wrong.

Just don’t forget: in a credible, professional review, you must use cause and effect to express your claims. Failing to describe what’s in your head confuses readers, and they’ll miss your real point. As mentioned, you should use the following skills to examine a game’s core framework:

- Description (depicting)
- Analysis (interpreting)
- Judgment (concluding)
- Recommendation (affirming the judgment)

By description we mean an appropriate, worthwhile portrayal of the section at hand. For instance, if you’re examining gameplay, you must first explain its mechanics and available features to the reader. These explanations come with limits, too. For example, you must never reveal major story points along the way.

After describing the game’s various parts, you must analyze how they’re implemented and how they function. You need a strong capacity for analysis to recognize the connections between subjects and to build a sound interpretation of every dimension of their existence into your review. How a feature is used, the logic behind it, the creators’ intent, similar examples, progress relative to previous installments or comparable games in the genre: all of these are open to interpretation.

If you describe the necessary dimensions correctly, the reader gains a proper working familiarity with the mechanics. Then, through analysis, you determine how well the player’s expectations and the development team’s and publisher’s marketing align with the actual realities of the game’s different parts.

Then comes judgment, the most important element in critiquing any section, and it matters enormously even in the smallest, least significant corners of a game. The groundwork you laid with the previous two elements must be aligned so that your conclusion about that section’s quality appears logical.

For example, when describing, analyzing, and interpreting one of the game’s characters (the cause), you cannot, after several lines of positive points and reasonable praise for the team, deliver a negative judgment (the effect, or consequence of that cause). Do that, and your judgment about one of the game’s mechanics, elements, or sections looks illogical and forfeits its credibility.

Sometimes, even after you’ve placed a coherent, perfectly appropriate analysis and judgment in your review, there’s still a chance that, from the player’s perspective, it lacks sufficient grounds for the verdict you reached. That’s when a recommendation lets you spell out how the creators could have done better, or at least won you over.

There are particular points to bear in mind for all these steps. Etch this one in your mind right now: when it comes to using the vocabulary, definitions, arguments, and specialized terminology tied to each part of a video game, don’t fall off either side of the roof. Neither fully jargon-laden usage nor a naive, oversimplified one is acceptable.

No critic can unpack every specialized topic in video games for readers unfamiliar with the field within a single review. Likewise, a critic isn’t expected to explain every dimension of those topics to the industry’s everyday audience either. Readers expect to understand the analyses and reasoning presented in a review.

The critic’s job is to meet that expectation, no less and no more. Using English terms, or worse, ill-fitting Persian equivalents, for a game’s various sections is part of this process. Ignore it, and you’ll face a chain of problems.

And what do we have up our sleeve for reviewing deconstructive works? Everyone enters that arena with their own weapon, and we suggest you make your perspective yours. Such games demand a level of skill above the standard bar of video game criticism.

A single yardstick doesn’t work the same way for works that have no peers, and many are the situations where finding a measure in this world, dark and colorful at once, becomes one of humanity’s endless torments. Close your eyes, open your mind. Reviewing deconstructive works deserves an article of its own. Until then, you can busy yourself with learning the fundamentals.

## The Structure of a Review

A proper review can be delivered as a written article, a video, or even a podcast: a key capability that non-standard reviews lack. In such standard reviews, repurposability is at its maximum. With modest edits to the text, you can move readers on any platform and guide them efficiently.

In fact, if your review has the necessary literary structure and coherent writing, it can compensate for other weaknesses, which we absolutely don’t recommend, but that fact alone shows how much polished, deliberately planned prose matters.

- Introduction
- Body (the review framework)
- Conclusion: summary, list of pros and cons, and the score

The division above is an excellent way to organize everything we’ve touched on so far. The introduction and conclusion are considered critically important, so much so that they cannot be stripped from a purposeful, literary piece of writing.

The body, the content, is where your examinations of the game live, and they must be carried out with the skills of criticism. We’ll introduce all of these sections in turn below. The order of the main structure, the introduction and conclusion, is expected to remain the same, but no particular writing order can be forced on anyone.

Worth noting: the order in which the sections of a review _appear_ often differs from the order in which they’re _written_, and quite a few seasoned critics start with whichever section feels most tangible to them.

If you can’t keep things orderly, or if the words refuse to leave your head no matter how hard you try, take inspiration from that pattern. If you’re not ready to evaluate the story, for instance, start with another section.

After reviewing your notes and applying your analytical skills, each word and phrase you pour onto the screen or page will summon more material to mind. Little by little, you’ll notice your experiences with the game storming your brain from every door and wall.

Don’t let them escape or hide in a corner. Connect them, piece by piece, to the review framework: story, gameplay, visuals, audio, and so on. Scattered focus and chaotic writing breed only more confusion, but with long-term practice you’ll be able to finish examining one section before moving to the next, giving a single part your full attention at a time.

Once the various sections of the article are ready, you can arrange them in the correct order and, with a round of edits, build the necessary connections and smooth transitions from one section to the next.

Note that orderly writing that respects the literary structure described above is the easiest and most common approach critics use, but believe me, you are the lawgiver here, and breaking the rules in different situations produces striking (if unpredictable) results. Next, we’ll walk through the literary structures, which, as it happens, contain the review framework within them.

### Introduction

Allow us to slap a bitter truth across your face: 90 percent of people go straight to a review’s conclusion. In written reviews, they immediately jump to the end of the article. In video reviews, after a few scene changes, they eventually skip to the final stretch of the video to see what’s what: how spectacularly, or how terribly, their game of interest performed!

So should we abandon cohesive writing and pour all our focus into the conclusion? Of course not! We were describing users’ first glance and split-second decisions, but plenty happens _after_ someone reads a conclusion.

If your conclusion is well organized, and alongside the pros and cons you’ve assigned the game a fair score, a portion of readers will be tempted to dig through your full text for deeper explanations.

Beyond those users who unconsciously return to the text and forget their hasty judgment, there’s another group that opens the page or video from the very start intending to read the whole review.

This whole process points to one thing: after the conclusion, the introduction receives more direct attention than any other part of the text. A lightbulb should now go on in your head: write an excellent conclusion, but for the introduction, settle for nothing short of an explosion!

Inject a gripping, unforgettable introduction into your text so that readers keep being drawn further in. Many professional critics confess that in every line, or at the start of every paragraph, they deploy elements that wield a magical power in the craft of writing to pull audiences in.

Doing this is one of those simple-but-complicated things we mentioned at the top of this article. But if you, too, have been skipping introductions, we have to burst your bubble: read the introduction. There will be no reminders!

Everyone uses particular methods not only for writing introductions but for writing and evaluating every part of a game. Those methods, like a person’s signature phrases, are extensions of their personality and become easier to spot after years of experience.

As you grow in video game criticism, you too will consciously and unconsciously develop your own rules, and their presence will be felt in your introductions. Still, even if you’ve already set a dozen such rules for yourself, try not to grow dogmatic about your assumptions!

Drawing on the background of the studio, publisher, or developers, expectations, previous installments, fascinating facts, and so on are all common ways to open a review. Providing extra context readers might need is another way to establish yourself in this section: a quick summary of the game’s genre, its core feature, and the like are familiar examples you’ve seen before. Posing a question and answering it over the course of the article or in the conclusion is another solid method.

Whatever you do, choose your introduction’s content according to the circumstances: the game, its popularity, and how well players know it and the topics around it. An introduction should not be crammed with long, technical explanations. The most engaging and most essential details are enough. Always remember: the introduction exists to hook the reader and prepare them to enter the piece.

You’re not expected to fit history’s greatest reference work into an introduction! Invite the audience in and get them ready to enter a new world of specialized criticism. Because these two sections, the introduction and conclusion, carry so much weight and do so much to set up the rest of the review, critics usually give them separate time and double the deliberation.

### Story, Plot, and Characterization

You don’t need an advanced degree in each corresponding field to examine the different parts of a video game. Plenty of critics work purely from personal experience and knowledge they’ve acquired by choice.

That’s why we stressed knowledge and growth at the start of this article. The story, plot, and characterization section houses concepts that demand substantial command of the subject to evaluate.

Given the difficulty of telling these things apart and the analysis this section demands, we might call it one of the least understood areas among amateur video game critics. In truth, swapping the word “unknown” for “misunderstood” conveys our meaning better. Using “story,” “storytelling,” “narrative,” and “plot” interchangeably is fundamentally wrong.

Story and plot have significant, glaring differences you must know. “Story” refers to a set of interconnected events, recounted and unfolding according to their sequence and the relationships between them.

“Plot” refers to a set of events whose relationship within the narration is a direct result of the events themselves. In other words, a plot is a set of events among which a cause-and-effect relationship can be observed.

To gauge the depth of characterization of in-game characters, you must weigh their goals, traits, and actions. Characterization, like the two elements above, is a far longer conversation than this article can contain. Trying to cover the differences between character types and a thousand other story elements is so unreasonably vast it would turn this piece into an endless ramble.

If you struggle with any of the terms above, searching the internet, reading relevant books, and rubbing shoulders with people of the pen will serve you well.

But even with reasonable skill here and the ability to parse story elements, you must not approach every game the same way. Some games don’t need a powerful story or characterization. Some developers use no defined story at all in their work, and analyzing and judging that is entirely different from judging a narrative work. Convention-breaking works won’t be laying all their rules neatly inside the bounds of your skills either. Always research first, make sure of your footing, and only then pass judgment.

Paying attention to dialogue, dialogue writing, and how they connect to the other sections is another vital facet of this part. Story, plot, and characterization relate directly and indirectly to everything else. For example, when examining set pieces, you must uncover and analyze those connections.

And while uncovering those connections, under no circumstances, not in description, analysis, judgment, or even recommendation, may major story beats be revealed. This skill alone is a genuine art: the critic is forced to deliver a compact analysis and a verdict that all read as logical, without spoiling the story or its strengths. Your job, then, is to speak about something the reader doesn’t know by default, and convince them with your words!

### Gameplay

In simple terms, gameplay is everything you can control and interact with. Games generally enlist story elements, visuals, and audio to dress up this section, the very essence of the medium.

As with the story section, examining gameplay through a fixed set of principles doesn’t always produce good results. Games have different aims. If a violent work doesn’t carry enough violence in its gameplay, the game is flawed, even though violence, on the whole, is not something welcomed in the real world. You must weigh the creators’ goals and the target audience when examining gameplay.

Have the gameplay’s features, capabilities, and mechanics been implemented properly? How do they entertain you, and in what state could they have done it better?

How, and how deeply, does the gameplay engage you? Examining these things differs fundamentally from game to game. The role of a weapon in a shooter differs from its role in a detective game, and if developers, beyond understanding those differences, also harness their potential correctly, the gameplay will deliver solid quality.

Likewise, the quantity and variety of gameplay are high priorities for developers. No developer expects players to grind through hours of repetitive gameplay. On the other hand, there’s no guarantee that developers can meet expectations even when they understand them, nor that repetitive gameplay is unsuitable for a work under all circumstances.

Level design also ranks among game development’s complex topics. For the umpteenth time, let us say: “You don’t need to be a game developer to critique a game,” but beyond being a player, a developer’s mindset must have taken root in you. If you can’t separately identify level design, camera angle, AI, the available mechanics, and their quality and quantity, then chase more and more knowledge.

Don’t toss comparison out of the equation, either. Comparison is a powerful tool for weighing the quality and quantity of a game’s different parts, especially gameplay, against previous entries in the series or similar titles in the genre. Games in a series usually make considerable strides with each installment, relative to the same developer’s or others’ earlier work. You must analyze and judge the function of that progress, its impact, and whether it was necessary.

Attention to difficulty, and then to the balance across all of these elements, matters too. Readers are entitled to the essential details about a game’s fairness, and to know just how successful the development team was at striking that balance.

Other factors that seem peripheral can play a far more important role than the most complex gameplay mechanics. For instance, how in-game purchases are handled and their effect on the game: all of it is fair game for examination. Server issues and small technical flaws that heavily shape your experience and the reader’s play a prominent role in a standard review.

### Visuals

Technical and artistic graphics form the two halves of a game’s visuals. Technical graphics cover textures, shadows, lighting, and so on. Some textures simply aren’t good enough. Other games display stunningly high-quality textures that, at release, stand as the best available point of comparison with reality, yet they load slowly.

The same problem applies to shadows and lighting. If one stretch of the game shows excellent shadow work and lighting, your work on the other stretches isn’t done. If the game features a day-night cycle, that lighting must remain appropriate and stable throughout the experience. At night, for instance, lamps, candles, and openings of light must be consistent with the game’s environmental design language.

How wide is the field of view? Are the far-off areas rendered as well as the ones ahead? Animations, physics, explosions, and the like are examined here too. Destructibility matters just as much, for holding the player’s attention and for its impact on the game and the player.

Graphics technologies, like everything else tech-related, advance daily. Motion capture has long lent a smoother, more convincing feel to main and supporting characters alike. The transfer of real people’s movements into the game must have been done flawlessly.

Ray Tracing and similar technologies like RTX must, in any case, claim their share of your review. If such visual technologies perform above the average of comparable examples, say so, and explain why. If they perform worse, let players know.

And while you’re neck-deep in graphics and technical talk, the game’s overall performance is also a fundamental issue that must be critiqued. A professional critic can detect frame rate drops.

A stable frame rate, an appropriate frame rate, and the target resolution across different platforms must all be examined, especially if you’re reviewing a remaster or enhanced version of an original title. Not only do these details gain added importance there, but the graphics discussion demands twice the scrutiny.

Only a precious few games hold onto their visual values over time. In other words, the technical and artistic graphics of some games are independent of their era and carry no expiration date.

You must know these games and dig out the reasons why. Typically, works that convey their messages and goals through gameplay, and that were capable of forcing fundamental change on the industry at launch, enjoy this quality. Without those conditions, you’d struggle to find many games on which time doesn’t take its usual toll.

Which brings us to artistic graphics. Artistic graphics encompass the game’s artistic design. How are the environment’s artistic elements arranged, and to what end? What colors have the creators used to convey the setting’s message or mood? Does the atmosphere pull you under completely, or does it quickly strain your eyes and tire your brain? What details have gone into the game’s visual presentation?

Analyzing, interpreting, and judging every part of a game’s visuals demands the same familiar criticism skills we devoted a huge chunk of this article to.

Look closely and you’ll see that adjectives like beautiful, ugly, fitting, unfitting, and so on come more readily to graphics than to any other section, so you must be on guard. Consider this a warning before you head down the wrong path. If you catch yourself leaning excessively and irrationally on adjectives instead of explanations, go back and spend time on the four core skills of criticism.

When analyzing a game’s technical and visual graphics, rank the design language first, then the developers’ goals and experience, and finally the expectations and fans of the target audience, high among your priorities. Pixel art, cel-shaded, realistic, fantasy design languages and the rest each carry different requirements. A critic, for instance, cannot fault a game’s pixel-art graphics for not being realistic, and that’s a textbook example of an unintentional mistake born of misunderstanding.

What’s more, if a game’s goal is properly understood, you can see the developers’ priorities. While many developers strive to deliver realistic graphics, they don’t come anywhere close to this industry’s big-budget AAA productions. When comparing, you must prioritize whether the game was purposeful and whether it achieved that purpose.

Countless teams with little or no experience have created enduring games despite weak technical graphics. If the graphics do no harm to the game as a whole, your complaints don’t hold up logically.

So: realistic graphics, higher-quality textures, better output resolution, advanced visual technologies, and so on do not necessarily make a better game, but purposeful graphics can. The user interface is part of this story too. The UI must convey all necessary information without cluttering the screen. In the least time and space, the most information, and the most important information, must sit before the player’s eyes.

You can also devote a passage to the game’s accessibility features. Not every game is expected to ship with comprehensive accessibility options, but now, in the ninth generation of video games, basics like attention to subtitles, the user interface, the ability to customize them, and so on should matter to both sides of the equation.

These things may rank very low in priority for developers, users, and especially critics, but if a company consistently provides them, you should pay attention. If, over time, that company regresses here instead of progressing, that’s not a good sign. You have before you an example of the development team not working purposefully, one whose ill effects may be visible in other parts of the game as well.

### Audio

Few games exist that, in a review, pull a meaningful share of critics toward their sound. As a result, audio usually gets only a brief, obligatory appearance in the shadow of the visuals.

Which is one more reason for you to pay closer attention to it! Plenty of reviews don’t bother, so there’s much to learn by doing the work yourself. Every sound employed in a game is part of its sound design.

From the sounds you hear in the user interface, to the protagonist’s voice, to the sound of rain striking the ground: all of it reflects the studio’s efforts to raise the quality of the audio. If you hear a bird in the distance, an audio element is playing in your ear that you must analyze and examine.

If that sound is used at dawn to wake enemies, it’s a successful effort; but if it comes from a bird that clearly doesn’t exist in the game’s world, it’s a major flaw. That is exactly the kind of analysis we’ve been talking about.

Used well, sound can play an even more important role than the graphics. Without it, it’s unlikely you’d so much as flinch at a horror game’s most terrifying scenes.

Beyond such immediately striking uses, similar elements won’t earn your notice as long as they behave in harmony. Trouble usually appears only when the sound design isn’t right. You’ve heard the saying: nobody praises you for doing things correctly, but everybody criticizes you for doing them wrong?

The quality and quantity of the sound applied to in-game features and mechanics, weapons for instance, must be implemented with care. The background music, even if it can’t crack your phone’s playlist outside the game, must convey the appropriate feel of each setting within it.

Proportionality is paramount. Thrilling music doesn’t suit a mournful scene. If the music fails to convey the right feeling, it has effectively worked against the game.

To that end, watch the lip sync and the characters’ animation. The depth of the voice work and a voice actor’s mastery in breathing life into a character shapes a large part of that character’s identity. A good voice actor, even saddled with bad dialogue and a brief presence, can leave more of an impact than characters with far more lines and screen time.

Don’t forget the sound effects. An explosion without its sound effect, even wrapped in the most intricate, most realistic graphics, hasn’t accomplished what it must. If a game has placed particular emphasis on its music, pay attention. If it fails, you must analyze that more thoroughly than usual.

### Fun Factor, Replay Value, and Innovation

As we said, a game’s progress and improvement over other installments in its series or over similar games matters. But this is an inherent, somewhat clichéd dynamic. Pure progress alone cannot turn a game into a good one, or, at minimum, its parts won’t clear the bar for complete success.

Imagine a mechanic, character, piece of music, or design element that feels superfluous and harmful in a game. If the developers refine it every single time, they make their game considerably worse with each iteration!

Even if that’s not the case, plenty of people have no interest in experiencing a “better” version of something they’ve already played. Or, conversely, even if many are content with minor improvements, we can’t ignore the potential of far more players who will join a game’s audience because of new capabilities and fresh features.

At this juncture, creativity, innovation, and new ideas arrive carrying a heavy load of claims, but often, many of those claims never get beyond the slogans, or die in the womb.

New ideas must be introduced and then deployed in service of heightening the fun factor and player immersion, or the developers’ other goals. If a new idea makes the game worse, we’d all be better off with a rerun of the previous game’s untouched formula!

The risk of fundamental change in beloved series, or even in games without multiple entries, is high. Keep your expectations reasonable. If a series razes its entire legacy to the ground with every installment, it swings the axe at the roots of all its users and fans.

But necessary innovations don’t require burning the heritage down. Developers who keep cashing in on their proven, successful formula cannot shrug off the burden of adding new ideas. Sooner or later, they must accept that truth and act on it. If that day has long since passed into history, call it out in your review, with sound reasoning.

With these ideas or without them, replay value is another element that can balance the equation in reviewing a game, or upset it entirely. Right now, online games boast enormous replayability and, as a major rival to titles lacking that capability, are seizing the market.

Games that don’t focus on this quality are gradually planning to raise their replay value. The grafting of role-playing elements onto titles that aren’t RPGs confirms the trend. If a video game can deliver high replay value in this turbulent market without resorting to repetitive, exhausting missions, you must tell your audience how, and to what extent.

Works that, contrary to this approach, resort to trickery to inflate replay value or even playtime haven’t increased replay value at all; they’ve cut its throat. Such an approach destroys the game’s fun factor. By drawing connections between the different sections, the critic must ultimately judge the fun factor.

Of course, a game can trade entertainment for purpose and idealism. If a game loudly declares that it wasn’t built to entertain people, you’re free to personally disagree with its creators, but you have no right to drag that disagreement into the review. In such cases the work must be judged on its purpose: does it fulfill the developers’ goals and the expectations of audiences who understand that purpose, and does it pursue its own ideals?

**Conclusion (Wrap-Up)**

Remember the introduction section? We said there that 90 percent of people go straight to the conclusion, but if you’ve put together a proper, sensible text, many will circle back to read the introduction and then the rest of the review.

The conclusion is a standard, unambiguous summary of everything you’ve addressed in the body of your review. Try to capture the game’s biggest positives and negatives in a few lines, without ambiguity, and without raising more questions.

Note that you cannot contradict your earlier statements here. A clash between the conclusion and the body of the review is like a broken equation, one whose two sides don’t match, though everyone expects them to.

Praise the efforts that deserve it, and scrutinize the ones that failed. Merely declaring that an effort has failed means you have failed to analyze it and provide a valid reason, and that’s a standard you must hold to across the entire review, not just the conclusion.

Do you hate the game’s genre, the developers, the publisher, the outlet, or even yourself and gaming? Not even slightly does it matter. Not for even one second should you consider letting personal hostility, hatred, or fandom seep into your review, or, worse, into its conclusion. The gods of the pen have revoked that license!

Anger, as an emotion, is something we need in moderation in life, but in a review, give precedence to your other feelings. Don’t sacrifice fairness and mercy on the altar of your personal opinions. For example, if you believe, and experience has proven, that a small bug or technical problem will be easily and quickly patched by the studio, don’t treat it mercilessly. Set aside everything that would unjustly color your judgment.

The studio’s harsh working conditions, information leaks, repeated delays, major changes during development, and so on are none of your business. In this courtroom, be judge and counsel at once, and let only the judge’s justice deliver the final word. If you can’t do that, please don’t enter the field of criticism! Description, analysis, judgment, and recommendation, covered earlier in this article, are your true friends.

Remember: everyone makes mistakes, and by writing an unfair review, you can commit a bigger one. This inner struggle usually intensifies when it’s time to assign the score. Before you do, identify the positive and negative points, the complete distillation of your review, and write them at the end of the conclusion.

Most critics assign a score based on their final feeling and an overall impression of the game, and Gamefa follows the same policy. Even so, weighing the positives and negatives can act as a guide, illuminating and marking the path for you. Use imagination to sketch both the dimensions that were real and the ones that could have been. Don’t believe in imagination? The image above _is_ the music, and if you’re a little curious, you’ll discover its secret even beyond your imagination.

Out of 5, out of 10, or out of 100? All of these express the same measure of quality through a simple conversion. So rather than falling into the trap of scores, take this as your creed: [numbers can never prove artistic value](https://gamefa.com/754498/game-critic-systems-limits/), but people can never move past numbers, either. A notion whose creator always stands exposed to the blade of criticism and other critics.

In the end, this is how you can write a review that reads as simple, graced with proper literary structure, fitting skill, and a coherent framework, while its complexities stay behind the curtain.

Readers, in that scenario, settle in to watch a dazzling display of criticism: a show in which your efforts to master the complexities of simplicity either finally pay off or plunge into the abyss of failure. Yet even in the latter case, you may soon witness a success drawn from the heart of these seemingly simple efforts. Have you forgotten? Simplicity is the ultimate sophistication, and in the words of Steve Jobs, “Simplicity can be harder than complexity.”

## What Do Gamefa’s Scores Mean?

### 10 – Masterpiece

A score of 10 means the work in question is a masterpiece and a part of video game history. These works typically exert enormous influence on the industry and push existing rules and standards forward. These games have the potential to be remembered in the future as some of the finest works of entertainment ever made.

Revolutionary, influential games usually rise up from this category to change history. Games that may not be perfect by every yardstick, but where the development team’s entire effort was exceptionally devoted to perfecting them, something you can feel in every particle of the work; a total effort to build something barely short of perfection. So a 10 doesn’t mean the game is flawless, but its problems are so inconsequential that dredging up the flaws cannot stand in the way of conveying a distinct, one-of-a-kind, and ultimately influential experience, to both players and the industry.

### 9 to 9.9 – Excellent

An unforgettable game that can make the list of the best works of its release year. These games usually have so few problems that it’s hard to fit them into a review and present them as notable issues. Such games have little room left for improvement, and many people, waving off that remaining potential, might even award them a 10. These games also have strong staying power and are sometimes recognized as cult classics.

### 8 to 8.9 – Very Good

Valuable works that can easily entertain users or fulfill their purpose. These products are high in quality, but their problems can by no means be overlooked or excused. Games in this category usually resemble other works in their genre, yet don’t come across as clichéd. And even when they are distinctive titles, they can’t always carve out a truly unique identity for themselves, but the development team has succeeded in realizing a great many of its goals.

### 7 to 7.9 – Good

Games that offer a decent, enjoyable experience, but whose problems, large and small, play on the player’s mind on par with the positives. The developers have made a genuine effort to achieve their goals, yet some of the most essential ones don’t pan out in practice and don’t produce satisfying results. Parts of such games could look far better with a series of improvements. Perhaps these games won’t be remembered in the future, or at least won’t be counted as the spark of positive or distinctive change, but a very large share of players will never regret playing them.

### 6 to 6.9 – Acceptable

These games perform only slightly better than average. Their strengths barely edge out their weaknesses. Usually the final product not only doesn’t aim to change or evolve the standards, it struggles even to meet the current ones. Playing these games will rarely add anything noteworthy to your life. Some gamers will probably enjoy them, but there’s no guarantee that a large crowd will be satisfied with the result.

### 5 to 5.9 - Mediocre

For every good point in these games, a bad point struts its stuff too. A large share of players will find themselves wrestling, over and over, with whether the game is any good and whether to keep playing, and those moments of doubt are usually numerous. The room for improvement is so vast you’ll struggle to believe the team put proper work into any part of it.

### 4.9 and Below – Poor or Terrible

It’s unlikely you’ll play these games for any reason other than to punish yourself! The core of these works sits very far from refinement and the desired bar; perhaps many of the necessary mechanics were never even implemented. You’d be hard-pressed to recommend these games to anyone. In short, these titles satisfy only a tiny handful of people. For your information: if you ever plan to make games, be sure to play these. Then, once you have, try never to make anything like them!
